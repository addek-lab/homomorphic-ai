Five open questions I'm tracking

Marketing around FHE tends to skip the uncomfortable parts. Here are the questions I think decide whether Homomorphic AI becomes mainstream, and how I plan to look into each.

1. How far can the performance gap close?

Encrypted inference is far slower than plaintext inference. The open question is whether algorithmic progress and hardware (GPUs, FPGAs, dedicated accelerators) bring that into a range where it is acceptable for real products, and for which model sizes. How I'll look: benchmark the same small models across libraries and hardware, as described in my lab setup.

2. What happens to non-linear layers?

Addition and multiplication are cheap by comparison. ReLU, softmax and attention are not. Polynomial approximations lose accuracy, and bootstrapping-based lookups cost time. How I'll look: measure the accuracy loss when swapping activations, layer by layer.

3. Can LLMs ever be practical?

Transformers combine huge matrix multiplications with softmax and layer norm, which is close to a worst case for FHE. Research on encrypted transformer inference exists, but I treat any "private LLM" claim cautiously until I see the model size, latency and hardware stated. How I'll look: read the recent encrypted-transformer papers with a focus on exactly those three numbers.

4. Who manages the keys?

FHE shifts the problem from "trust the server" to "protect the secret key and the client". Key generation, rotation, and what happens on a compromised device are less glamorous, and just as important. Evaluation keys can also be large. How I'll look: map out the full key lifecycle for a realistic deployment.

5. FHE or confidential computing?

Trusted execution environments (TEEs) run at near-native speed but make you trust the hardware vendor and its attestation. FHE relies on mathematics alone but costs far more compute. For many real systems the answer is probably a hybrid. How I'll look: write the threat model for three example workloads and see which approach each one really needs.

If you work on any of this and I've got something wrong, I'd like to hear it. Wrong beliefs are the cheapest thing to fix early.
